## The core tension the brand lives in
"Goblin" + "AI for education" is a deliberate mismatch. AI is usually sold as either hype (magic, revolutionary) or threat (replacing teachers, cheating). This brand instead positions AI as a **practical, slightly mischievous tool** — something you tinker with, not something that overwhelms you. The goblin is a trickster who's actually on your side: irreverent about the hype, serious about the pedagogy.

This is NOT a corporate AI-consultancy brand (no "synergy," no generic blue-gradient SaaS look) and NOT a cutesy ed-tech brand (no cartoon mascots, no primary-color playfulness aimed at children — the audience is adult educators).

## Voice
- Practical over hyped. Every claim should be groundable in a real workshop, a real school, a real outcome.
- Warm but not soft — confident from two decades of classroom experience, not from AI enthusiasm.
- Human-centered explicitly: AI is discussed as *guided autonomy*, not automation. The recurring philosophical anchor is "AI amplifies human judgment, it doesn't replace it."
- Bilingual as a first-class citizen, not an afterthought — Hebrew and English content should feel equally native, not like one is a translation of the other.

## Audience
Primary: school leaders, teachers, CPD coordinators, college administrators (Kaye College, Mofet Institute network) — people evaluating whether to bring Idan in for training.
Secondary: NGOs, community organizations, individuals (older adults, job seekers) attending public workshops.
Both audiences are non-technical. Design should never feel like a developer-tool or startup landing page — it should feel like it was made by someone who understands classrooms, not just code.

## Visual direction v2 — "dark glassmorphism meets arcane tech"
Superseded: the original brief called for light-mode-first with a restrained blue accent. Idan has since confirmed he wants the site to actually look like the Goblin Productions mark — a unique, dark, fantasy-cyberpunk identity, not a generic light SaaS template. The bridge between "credible for school administrators" and "actually matches the logo" is the **2026 dark-glassmorphism trend**: moody dark-mode UI, translucent frosted-glass panels, and ambient neon gradients used with restraint rather than as loud gamer-UI decoration.

- **Dark-mode-first.** Near-black/deep-charcoal base (not pure #000), not white. This is now the primary identity, not an optional toggle.
- **Two-tone neon accent system**, taken straight from the reference art: teal/cyan as the primary accent (links, active states, primary buttons), violet/magenta as a secondary accent (highlights, gradients, hover glows). Small, deliberate hits of green as a tertiary spark, not a third dominant color.
- **Glass panels, not flat cards.** Case-study cards, nav bar, and content sections use subtle frosted-glass surfaces (translucent dark fill, soft blur, thin light-catching border) rather than the flat white cards from v1.
- **Rune/gear motifs as texture, not toys.** Thin-line circular framing, subtle rune-like glyphs, or gear outlines can appear as low-opacity background texture or section dividers — echoing the logo's circular badge frame — but must stay decorative background noise, never competing with real content or reading as a children's mascot.
- **Ambient ombre glow, used sparingly.** One or two soft glowing color orbs positioned behind hero content (per the dark-glassmorphism pattern) is enough; avoid tiling neon everywhere or the site reads as a gaming site instead of a consultancy.
- **Photography still real and central** — Idan's actual photos (portrait, workshops, Oxford badge) should sit inside glass-panel frames with a thin neon-edge glow, grounding the fantasy palette in a real, credible person.
- **Structured storytelling for case studies retained** — Challenge → Approach → Result cards, now styled as glass panels with a small rune/gear accent rather than flat white boxes.
- **Motion**: soft glow pulses and hover-lift on glass panels are in-theme now (unlike v1's "subtle only" rule) — but keep it restrained: no particle effects, no constant animation, nothing that fights readability for a non-technical audience.
- **Typography stays legible-first**: a clean sans body font; the serif/display accent from v1 can be swapped for a sharper, slightly geometric display face if it reads more "tech-fantasy," but must remain easily readable for older/non-technical visitors — no faux-rune display fonts for body or headings.

## What to avoid
- Going full gaming/RPG UI — no health-bar-style borders, no loot-box aesthetics, no fantasy-game HUD chrome. The reference art sets the palette and mood, not a literal game skin.
- Cutesy/childish goblin mascot illustrations used as a recurring character — the reference art is a mood/palette source, not a mascot to place on every page.
- Neon overload — if every section glows, nothing feels special. Reserve strong glow for hero and key CTAs.
- Corporate consultancy sterility — stock photos of people shaking hands, generic "solutions" language.
- Treating Hebrew as a mirrored afterthought of the English copy — RTL layout needs real design attention, not just `direction: rtl`. Glass-panel and glow effects must mirror correctly in RTL too.
- Sacrificing legibility for mood — text contrast on dark glass panels must stay comfortably readable, especially for older workshop attendees who are a real part of the audience.
